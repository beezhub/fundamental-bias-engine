## Trading plan

### 1. Goals and Objectives

- Aim to preserve capital and grow the account steadily over time.
- Focus on high-probability trade setups with favorable risk-reward ratios.
- Limit the risk per trade to a maximum of 1% - 2% of the account balance (R20 - R40).

### 2. Risk Management

- As a small account, prioritize capital preservation. Limit the risk per trade to 1% - 2% of the account balance to protect against significant losses.
- Always use a stop-loss order, placed just beyond the opposite side of the trend channel or key support/resistance level.
- Adjust position sizes based on the distance between the entry price and the stop-loss level to ensure adherence to the 1% - 2% risk rule.

### 3. Trading Strategy

- Utilize trendlines and trend channels to identify key support and resistance levels and to determine the trend direction.
- Focus on higher probability setups and avoid chasing trades with low certainty.

### 4. Currency Pairs and Timeframes

- Trade currency pairs with low spreads and trading costs to optimize the value of each trade.
- Focus on major currency pairs (e.g., EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY) for their liquidity and popularity.
- Use 1-hour and 4-hour charts for trendline and trend channel analysis and trade execution.

### 5. News and Economic Events

- Avoid trading during high-impact news events to minimize exposure to excessive volatility and unpredictable price movements.
- Consider checking the economic calendar regularly to be aware of potential news events that may impact your trades.

### 6. Entry and Exit Rules

- Enter a long position when the price breaks above a descending trendline, and a higher low confirms the trend reversal.
- Enter a short position when the price breaks below an ascending trendline, and a lower high confirms the trend reversal.
- Set the take-profit level at a reasonable distance from the entry point, considering recent price action and support/resistance levels.
- Place the stop-loss order just beyond the trendline or key support/resistance level.

## Exit Strategies

### 1. Take-Profit Orders

- Set a predetermined take-profit level for each trade based on your risk-reward ratio and the available profit potential.
- Be realistic with profit targets, considering the limited capital in the account.

### 2. Trailing Stop Loss

- Use a trailing stop-loss order to lock in gains as the trade moves in your favor.
- Adjust the trailing distance based on the volatility of the market and the timeframe you are trading.

### 3. Support/Resistance Levels

- Take partial profits or consider fully closing the trade when the price reaches a key support or resistance level identified by previous swing highs or lows.

### 4. Trendline and Trend Channel Breaks

- Exit the trade if the price breaks beyond the opposite side of the trend channel or key trendline, as it may indicate a potential trend reversal.

### 5. Time-Based Exits

- Set a predetermined time frame for each trade. If the trade doesn't reach the take-profit level or shows signs of stalling, consider closing the trade before the set time expires.

Always maintain discipline in executing your trading plan and manage your risk
effectively. With a small account, focus on continuous learning and refining
your skills to prepare for potential account growth in the future. Be patient,
stay disciplined, and adapt your trading plan as needed based on your
performance and market observations.

## High Probability Setup guidelines

### 1. Trendline Rules

- Draw trendlines connecting at least two significant swing lows in an uptrend or two swing highs in a downtrend.
- For an uptrend, the trendline should have a positive slope, while for a downtrend, it should have a negative slope.
- The more touches (bounces) of the price on the trendline, the stronger it becomes as a support or resistance level.
- Consider using logarithmic charts for more accurate trendline placement, especially in trending markets.

### 2. Trend Channel Rules

- A trend channel is formed by drawing two parallel lines that encompass the price action within the trend.
- The upper line (resistance) is drawn by connecting the swing highs, while the lower line (support) connects the swing lows.
- The price should respect both the upper and lower channel lines, and bounces from these lines can signal potential trading opportunities.

### 3. Entry Rules

- Look for price action signals, such as candlestick patterns or bullish/bearish engulfing bars, near the trendline or channel lines. These can serve as confirmation for entry.
- Wait for the price to approach the trendline or channel before considering a trade. Avoid chasing the price far away from these levels.
- If the price breaks through the trendline or channel, wait for a retest of the broken line to confirm the validity of the breakout before considering a trade in the direction of the breakout.

### 4. Trade Management Rules

- Set clear stop-loss levels just beyond the opposite side of the trendline or channel to protect against potential losses if the price moves against your trade.
- Use multiple take-profit targets to secure profits at different price levels or consider trailing your stop loss as the price moves in your favor.
- Be mindful of major support and resistance levels that could impact your trade. Consider taking partial profits or adjusting your position size near these levels.

### 5. Risk Management Rules

- Stick to your maximum risk per trade, such as 1% - 2% of your account balance, to preserve capital and manage potential drawdowns.
- Adjust position sizes based on the distance between the entry price and the stop-loss level to align with your risk management guidelines.

### 6. Market Conditions

- Consider the overall market conditions and the presence of clear trends before implementing your trendline and channel strategy. This strategy is most effective in trending markets.

## Enhance your focus on high-probability setups

1. **Clearly Define Your Setup Criteria:** Develop a clear and specific set of criteria for identifying high-probability trade setups. These criteria could include specific patterns or combinations of technical indicators that signal potential entry points.
2. **Use Multiple Timeframes:** Analyze the market on multiple timeframes to confirm the validity of your setup. For example, if you're using the 1-hour chart for entry signals, check the 4-hour and daily charts for supporting trends and confluence.
3. **Wait for Confirmation:** Avoid jumping into trades prematurely. Wait for confirmation of your setup criteria before entering a trade. This could be a candlestick pattern, a breakout of a trendline, or a bounce from a key support/resistance level.
4. **Consider Confluence:** Look for confluence between different technical factors. For example, if a trendline coincides with a significant Fibonacci level or a moving average, it strengthens the setup's potential.
5. **Assess Market Context:** Consider the overall market context before taking a trade. Is the market trending strongly, consolidating, or ranging? High-probability setups often align with the prevailing market context.
6. **Avoid Trading in Choppy Markets:** In choppy or uncertain market conditions, high-probability setups may be less reliable. Focus on trading during clear trending phases.
7. **Trade with the Trend:** Trading in the direction of the prevailing trend increases the likelihood of success. Look for setups that align with the dominant trend on higher time frames.
8. **Be Selective:** Avoid overtrading by being selective about the setups you trade. Not every price movement will present a high-probability opportunity, so wait for the best ones.
9. **Learn from Past Trades:** Analyze your past trades to identify common characteristics of winning trades. Use this knowledge to refine your setup criteria and improve your trading decisions.
10. **Practice Patience:** Sometimes the best trades are the ones you don't take. Practice patience and discipline in waiting for setups that meet your criteria and offer a favorable risk-reward ratio.
11. **Avoid Revenge Trading:** After a losing trade, resist the urge to immediately enter another trade to "recover" losses. Stick to your trading plan and only take setups that meet your criteria.
12. **Stay Emotionally Detached:** Emotions can cloud judgment and lead to impulsive decisions. Stay emotionally detached from your trades and focus on objective analysis.

## Confirmation Patterns

### 1. Double Bottom and Double Top

- **Double Bottom:** This pattern forms after a downtrend and consists of two consecutive troughs (swing lows) that are roughly equal. It suggests a potential trend reversal to the upside.
- **Double Top:** This pattern forms after an uptrend and consists of two consecutive peaks (swing highs) that are approximately at the same level. It signals a potential trend reversal to the downside.
- **Entry:** Enter a long trade when the price breaks above the neckline of a double bottom pattern, or enter a short trade when the price breaks below the neckline of a double top pattern.

### 2. Head and Shoulders and Inverse Head and Shoulders

- **Head and Shoulders:** This pattern typically forms after an uptrend and consists of three peaks - the middle peak (the head) is higher than the two surrounding peaks (the shoulders). It indicates a potential trend reversal to the downside.
- **Inverse Head and Shoulders:** This pattern forms after a downtrend and is the opposite of the head and shoulders pattern. It consists of three troughs, with the middle trough (the head) lower than the surrounding troughs (the shoulders). It signals a potential trend reversal to the upside.
- **Entry:** Enter a short trade when the price breaks below the neckline of a head and shoulders pattern, or enter a long trade when the price breaks above the neckline of an inverse head and shoulders pattern.

### 3. Ascending and Descending Triangles

- **Ascending Triangle:** This pattern forms when the price creates higher lows and encounters a horizontal resistance level. It suggests a potential bullish breakout.
- **Descending Triangle:** This pattern forms when the price creates lower highs and encounters a horizontal support level. It suggests a potential bearish breakout.
- **Entry:** Enter a long trade when the price breaks above the upper trendline of an ascending triangle, or enter a short trade when the price breaks below the lower trendline of a descending triangle.

## Confirmation Candle Sticks

### Doji

A Doji is a candlestick pattern that occurs when the opening and closing prices
are nearly equal. It looks like a cross or a plus sign. It indicates indecision
in the market and suggests a potential trend reversal. There are different types
of Doji, including Dragonfly Doji, which has a long lower shadow, indicating a
possible bullish reversal.

### Engulfing Pattern

Bullish Engulfing - The Bullish Engulfing pattern is a two-candle pattern where
a small bearish candle is followed by a larger bullish candle that completely
engulfs the previous candle's body. It suggests a potential bullish trend
reversal, as the buyers have overwhelmed the sellers.

### Piercing Line

The Piercing Line is a two-candle pattern found at the end of a downtrend. It
consists of a bearish candle followed by a bullish candle. The bullish candle
opens below the previous close but closes above the midpoint of the previous
bearish candle. It indicates a potential bullish reversal.

### Harami Pattern

Bullish Harami - The Bullish Harami is a two-candle pattern that occurs during a
downtrend. It consists of a large bearish candle followed by a smaller bullish
candle. The bullish candle's body is completely contained within the bearish
candle's body. It suggests a potential bullish reversal.

### Hammer

The Hammer is a single candlestick pattern that has a small body near the top of
the candle and a long lower shadow. It often appears after a downtrend and
indicates a potential bullish reversal. It shows that buyers were able to push
the price higher from the lows.

### Spinning Top

The Spinning Top is a candlestick pattern with a small body and long upper and
lower shadows. It indicates indecision in the market between buyers and sellers.
It does not provide a clear direction but suggests a possible trend reversal.

### Bullish Railway Track Pattern

This pattern is formed by two consecutive bullish candles with similar or very
close opening and closing prices. They should have long lower shadows and
ideally open at or near the previous candle's low. The pattern suggests a
potential bullish reversal.

## Economic Events To Avoid

1. **Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP) Report:** This is a key employment report released by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics on the first Friday of each month. It provides data on the number of new jobs added to the U.S. economy, the unemployment rate, and average hourly earnings.
2. **Interest Rate Decisions:** Central banks, such as the Federal Reserve (FOMC), European Central Bank (ECB), Bank of England (BoE), and Bank of Japan (BoJ), announce their interest rate decisions and monetary policy statements at scheduled meetings. These announcements can significantly impact currency pairs.
3. **Gross Domestic Product (GDP) Reports:** GDP reports provide insights into the economic growth of a country or region. GDP data is released quarterly or annually, depending on the country.
4. **Consumer Price Index (CPI) and Producer Price Index (PPI):** These inflation indicators track changes in the prices of goods and services for consumers and producers, respectively. High inflation data can affect central bank policy decisions.
5. **Retail Sales Reports:** Retail sales data measures consumer spending and is a crucial indicator of economic health. It is released monthly in many countries.
6. **Employment Reports (UK and Canada):** Besides the U.S. NFP report, employment reports from other major economies like the UK (Claimant Count Change, Average Earnings Index) and Canada (Employment Change, Unemployment Rate) can also impact currency pairs.
7. **Trade Balance Reports:** These reports show the difference between a country's exports and imports and can influence the value of its currency.
8. **Central Bank Speeches and Press Conferences:** Statements and speeches made by central bank officials, including the heads of major central banks, can provide insights into future monetary policy decisions.
9. **FOMC Minutes and ECB Monetary Policy Meeting Accounts:** These releases provide detailed minutes from the Federal Reserve and European Central Bank meetings, respectively, offering additional insights into policy decisions.
10. **Geopolitical Events and Summit Meetings:** Significant political events, such as elections, referendums, and summit meetings, can impact the markets, especially when they concern major economies or political alliances.

## Keep in mind

1. **Stay Disciplined:** Discipline is crucial for successful trading. Stick to your trading plan, follow your rules, and avoid impulsive decisions based on emotions or external factors.
2. **Avoid Overtrading:** Trading too frequently or on low-probability setups can lead to losses. Be patient and wait for high-quality opportunities.
3. **Practice Patience:** Forex trading is not a get-rich-quick scheme. It requires time and effort to develop skills and achieve consistent profitability.
4. **Backtest and Demo Trade:** Before risking real money, backtest your strategy on historical data and practice on demo accounts to gain confidence and refine your approach.
5. **Avoid Chasing Losses:** After a losing trade, avoid the temptation to take excessive risks to recoup losses quickly. Stick to your risk management plan.
6. **Stay Informed:** Keep up with financial news and events that could impact the markets. Being informed helps you make better trading decisions.
7. **Continuous Learning:** Stay open to learning and improving your trading skills. Attend webinars, read books, and seek knowledge from experienced traders.
8. **Keep It Simple:** Avoid overcomplicating your trading strategy with too many indicators or complex rules. Simple strategies are often more effective.
9. **Understand Leverage:** If you use leverage, be aware of its potential to magnify both gains and losses. Use leverage judiciously and within your risk tolerance.
10. **Trade Size Matters:** Be mindful of your position sizes relative to your account balance. Avoid over-leveraging and risking too much on a single trade.
11. **Take Breaks:** Trading can be mentally and emotionally demanding. Take regular breaks to avoid burnout and maintain a clear perspective.
12. **Avoid Revenge Trading:** After a losing trade, take time to analyze what went wrong instead of immediately jumping into another trade out of frustration.
13. **Be Realistic:** Set achievable goals and have realistic expectations. Forex trading requires dedication and may not lead to overnight success.
14. **Track Your Progress:** Monitor your trading performance over time. Analyze your successes and failures to identify patterns and improve your strategy.
15. **Consider Multiple Perspectives:** Don't solely rely on technical analysis. Consider incorporating fundamental analysis and market sentiment in your decision-making.
16. **Be Ready for Drawdowns:** Even the best traders experience drawdowns. Prepare mentally and financially to endure periods of losses.

## Daily routine

### 1. Morning Routine

- Start your day by reviewing the financial news and economic calendar to be aware of any high-impact events scheduled for the day.
- Check the performance of any open positions from the previous day and adjust stop-loss or take-profit levels if necessary.
- Review your trading plan and any potential trade setups that may have emerged overnight.

### 2. Pre-Market Analysis

- Analyze the major currency pairs and other instruments you trade. Look for potential high-probability trade setups based on your strategy and technical analysis.
- Identify key support and resistance levels, trendlines, and trend channels on different timeframes.
- Consider the overall market sentiment and any fundamental factors that might influence your trades.

### 3. Trading Session

- Focus on trading during the hours when your chosen currency pairs or markets are most active and liquid.
- Wait for your identified trade setups to align with your entry criteria before entering any new trades.
- Avoid trading during high-impact economic releases or other news events.

### 4. Trade Management

- Once you enter a trade, set stop-loss and take-profit levels based on your risk management plan and the technical structure of the trade.
- If the trade moves in your favor, consider using a trailing stop to lock in profits as the price continues to move in your direction.
- Continuously monitor your trades and remain attentive to any potential changes in market conditions that may require adjustments to your positions.

### 5. Post-Market Analysis

- After the trading session ends, review the outcome of your trades for the day.
- Analyze the reasons behind both winning and losing trades to identify areas for improvement.
- Take note of any mistakes made and think about how to avoid them in future trades.

### 6. Learning and Improvement

- Spend time each day on continuous learning. Read trading books, watch educational videos, or attend webinars to improve your trading knowledge and skills.
- Participate in online trading communities or forums to exchange ideas with other traders and gain insights into different trading approaches.

### 7. Evening Routine

- Before ending your trading day, update your trading journal with the details of the trades you executed and the lessons learned from the day's trading activity.
- Evaluate your overall performance and assess whether you followed your trading plan and adhered to your risk management rules.

### 8. Rest and Recuperation

- Ensure you get enough rest and sleep to maintain mental clarity and avoid burnout.
- Remember that trading requires emotional resilience, so taking care of your physical and mental well-being is crucial for long-term success.

## Stays fully manual

The engine produces a directional lean and a conviction. It never produces a
trade. These remain entirely with the trader:

- **Drawing trendlines and trend channels** (High Probability Setup 1 and 2). Swing-point selection is a judgement call the engine does not attempt.
- **Candlestick and pattern confirmation** (Confirmation Patterns, Confirmation Candle Sticks). The engine reads no price bars for pattern recognition.
- **Entry timing** (Trading plan 6, High Probability Setup 3). Break, retest, higher low, lower high: all read off the 1h and 4h charts by eye.
- **Choosing the stop and target levels** (Exit Strategies 1, 3, 4). The risk module sizes a position from a stop the trader supplies. It does not decide where the stop belongs.
- **Trailing the stop and taking partial profits** (Exit Strategies 2, 3; High Probability Setup 4).
- **Deciding whether the market is trending or choppy** (Enhance your focus 5, 6; High Probability Setup 6).
- **The decision to take or skip any given trade.** The engine's output is one input to that decision.

## Deliberately out of scope

These are left alone on purpose, not through omission:

- **Order execution.** The engine never connects to a broker, places, modifies, or closes an order. There is no execution code path and none is planned.
- **Backtested performance claims.** No file in this repository asserts an edge that has not been measured on the owner's own journal. Until Phase 6 in `docs/roadmap.md` produces data, every weight in the config is a prior, not a finding.
- **Time-based exits** (Exit Strategies 5). The engine has no view on holding period. It refreshes a bias; it does not tell a trade to close.
- **Position size beyond the plan's cap.** No leverage optimisation, no martingale, no recovery sizing. `RiskConfig` caps risk per trade and total concurrent exposure and does nothing clever.
- **Anything outside G10.** The plan calls for low-spread majors. The universe is fixed at the eight G10 currencies in `src/fbe/universe.py`, and the ZAR account currency is used only for sizing arithmetic, never traded.
- **Psychology and routine.** "Stay Disciplined", "Take Breaks", "Practice Patience", "Avoid Revenge Trading". Software cannot enforce these. The journal makes lapses visible after the fact, which is the most it can honestly claim.
